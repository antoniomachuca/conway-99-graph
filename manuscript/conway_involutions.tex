\begin{abstract}
Conway's 99-graph problem asks whether a strongly regular graph with parameters $(99,14,1,2)$ exists. Cited results restrict the prime divisors of the automorphism group order to $\{2, 3\}$. This note records a Lean~4 library, SAT compilers, and solver logs, and it uses a four-state taxonomy---\textsc{Proved}, \textsc{Compiled}, \textsc{Explored}, and \textsc{Pending}---to separate kernel-checked theorems, checker-certified formula refutations, tested compilers, and open claims. A SAT claim is \textsc{Proved} only when the cited CNF is the file that was checked, its solver log ends in \texttt{s UNSATISFIABLE}, and \texttt{drat-trim} reports \texttt{s VERIFIED} on that pair.
In Lean~4, we kernel-verify core structural invariants: $K_4$-freeness, 1-factor neighborhood decomposition $7K_2$, diameter at most 2, and decidability of the adjacency matrix equation. These proofs depend solely on the foundational axioms \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}. We also formalize the arithmetic contradictions that underlie character-theoretic obstructions. This distinguishes verified arithmetic deductions from unverified geometric action hypotheses.
We also kernel-verify the first step of an orbit-matrix argument for order 7. Assume that $A$ satisfies the adjacency conditions and that a nontrivial permutation $s$ with $s^7 = \mathrm{id}$ preserves $A$. Then the Lean proof constructs a $15 \times 15$ orbit quotient matrix in a canonical order. The proof derives the fixed vertex, the orbit structure, and the quotient equations from these hypotheses; it does not assume them. Lean does not yet prove that no such matrix exists. The Lean statement that excludes order 7 therefore still depends on \texttt{sorryAx}.
For propositional logic, we implement canonical SAT encodings with Crawford symmetry-breaking predicates. A deterministic regression suite of 101 tests checks compiler components, geometric orbits, the supervisor lifecycle, positive controls, and an orbit-matrix encoder. The positive controls apply the production compilers for two actions to two known graphs with $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = 2$: Paley(9) and the Berlekamp--van Lint--Seidel graph on 243 vertices. These checks give \textsc{Compiled} status. They do not prove that a compiler is correct.
CaDiCaL 1.9.5 returned \texttt{s UNSATISFIABLE} on the repaired order-7 CNF ($177{,}097$ variables, $422{,}508$ clauses) in $58{,}054.87$\,s process time. It wrote a $26{,}746{,}488{,}053$-byte DRAT trace. \texttt{drat-trim} reported \texttt{s VERIFIED} in $103{,}482.837$\,s ($12{,}821{,}576{,}879$ resolution steps, $378{,}191$ core clauses, 0 RAT lemmas). That pair is \textsc{Proved}. Both files are deposited in Zenodo record \texttt{10.5281/zenodo.22995101}~\cite{conway99_zenodo}. The deposit does not decide the Thakkar--Severini CP-SAT search.
The note also records the column-weight identity $CC^T = 10I + 2J$ for the open involution case $f=1$, with corrected branch supports, and a 12.5-day CaDiCaL campaign that accumulated more than 2.4 billion conflicts without a satisfying assignment or a refutation. All code, Lean modules, and test suites are available at \url{https://github.com/antoniomachuca/conway-99-graph}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Literature Attribution}

A strongly regular graph $\mathrm{srg}(v, k, \lambda, \mu)$ is a regular graph on $v$ vertices with degree $k$. Every pair of adjacent vertices has exactly $\lambda$ common neighbors. Every pair of distinct non-adjacent vertices has exactly $\mu$ common neighbors. Whether a strongly regular graph with parameters $(99, 14, 1, 2)$ exists remains open~\cite{brouwer2011spectra,cesarz2025automorphisms}. Conway included the question among five problems carrying a \$1{,}000 prize. The circulated statement is the prize list \emph{Five \$1,000 Problems (Update 2017)}~\cite{conway1970}, Problem~2. Bill Cheswick added an update in 2014. Cheswick and Sloane added a postscript in 2017. That document is not a 1970 research article and it does not identify itself as conference proceedings.

Let $A \in \{0,1\}^{99 \times 99}$ denote the adjacency matrix of a Conway 99-graph. The strongly regular parameters require $A$ to satisfy the matrix equation
\begin{equation}
 A = A^T, \qquad \operatorname{diag}(A) = 0, \qquad A^2 + A = 12I_{99} + 2J_{99},
 \label{eq:matrix}
\end{equation}
where $I_n$ is the $n \times n$ identity matrix and $J_n$ is the $n \times n$ all-ones matrix. Spectral graph theory gives the eigenvalues of $A$: $k = 14$ (multiplicity 1), $r = 3$ (multiplicity 54), and $s = -4$ (multiplicity 44):
\begin{equation}
 \Spec(A) = \{14^1, 3^{54}, (-4)^{44}\}.
 \label{eq:spectrum}
\end{equation}
For each vertex $x \in V(G)$, let $\Gamma(x)$ denote its open neighborhood. Let $\Gamma_2(x) = V(G) \setminus (\{x\} \cup \Gamma(x))$ denote the second subconstituent. Because $k = 14$ and $\lambda = 1$, each vertex in $\Gamma(x)$ has exactly one neighbor in $\Gamma(x)$. The induced subgraph $G[\Gamma(x)]$ is therefore a 1-regular graph of seven disjoint edges ($7K_2$). The second subconstituent $\Gamma_2(x)$ contains $99 - 1 - 14 = 84$ vertices. Because $\mu = 2$, each vertex $y \in \Gamma_2(x)$ is adjacent to exactly two vertices in $\Gamma(x)$~\cite{brouwer2011spectra,cesarz2025automorphisms}.

\subsection{Literature Attribution of Automorphism Group Bounds}

An unconstrained combinatorial search on a 99-vertex graph involves $2^{\binom{99}{2}} = 2^{4851}$ possible edge configurations. Computational searches in this note therefore restrict attention to graphs that admit a nontrivial automorphism. Such a restriction cannot find a rigid graph. Prior literature establishes strict bounds on the automorphism group $\Aut(G)$:

\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Involutions (order 2) fix exactly one vertex.}
 Makhnev and Minakova (2004)~\cite{makhnev2001automorphisms} classified prime-order automorphisms of strongly regular graphs with $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = 2$. Makhnev's 2009 slides~\cite{makhnev2009symmetric}, attributing the result to Makhnev--Minakova, state that an involution of a Conway 99-graph fixes exactly one vertex.

 \item \textbf{Automorphisms of order 3 are fixed-point-free; orders 6 and 9 are excluded.}
 Behbahani and Lam (2011)~\cite{behbahani2011strongly} proved that an automorphism of order 3 on $\mathrm{srg}(99,14,1,2)$ has no fixed points. It acts as a fixed-point-free (FPF) permutation with 33 cycles of length 3. Crnkovi{\'c} and Maksimovi{\'c} (2020)~\cite{crnkovic2014strongly} proved that $\Aut(G)$ contains no subgroups of order 6 or 9. This result excludes $\mathbb{Z}_6, S_3, \mathbb{Z}_9$, and $\mathbb{Z}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_3$.

 \item \textbf{Prime orders $p \ge 5$ are excluded.}
 Behbahani and Lam (2011)~\cite{behbahani2011strongly} showed that the only possible prime divisors of $|\Aut(G)|$ are 2 and 3. They excluded automorphisms of order 5, 7, and higher primes through computer-assisted orbit matrix enumeration. Cesarz and Woldar (2025)~\cite{cesarz2025automorphisms} gave computer-free proofs that:
 \begin{equation}
  2 \mid |\Aut(G)| \implies |\Aut(G)| \mid 6,
  \qquad
  7 \mid |\Aut(G)| \implies \Aut(G) \cong \mathbb{Z}_7.
  \label{eq:prior_bounds}
 \end{equation}
 Combining this even-order implication with the exclusion of order 6 gives: if $2 \mid |\Aut(G)|$, then $|\Aut(G)| = 2$. Therefore, $\Aut(G)$ contains no elements of order 4 or 6. This is a consequence of the cited papers, not a new group-order theorem.

 \item \textbf{Computational benchmark context.}
 Thakkar and Severini (2026)~\cite{thakkar2026forced} encode forced-structure reductions and prescribed automorphisms in Google OR-Tools CP-SAT. They report that the single-fixed-point order-7 instance returned \texttt{UNKNOWN} after 48 hours on 14 cores. Their paper still treats a $\mathbb{Z}_7$-symmetric graph as open. A CNF refutation is a certificate for that instance only when the two formulations are shown to define the same set of graphs. This note does not give that correspondence.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{itemize}

 \item In Lean~4, we verify structural invariants ($K_4$-freeness, 1-factor neighborhood decomposition, diameter at most 2) and the decidability of the adjacency conditions. The Lean kernel checks these proofs using only \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}. We also formalize the arithmetic contradictions that arise in character-theoretic analyses.
 \item In Lean~4, we prove that a Conway 99-graph with a nontrivial automorphism $s$ such that $s^7 = \mathrm{id}$ gives a canonical $15 \times 15$ orbit quotient matrix (\texttt{conway\_z7\_induces\_orbit\_matrix}). The kernel reports only \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}. The orbit structure is known from the literature~\cite{behbahani2011strongly,cesarz2025automorphisms}. The contribution is a mechanized derivation. The nonexistence of such a matrix remains \textsc{Pending} in Lean.
 \item We implement canonical SAT compilers with Crawford symmetry-breaking predicates~\cite{crawford1996symmetry}.  \item We prove that every column of a binary $7\times 42$ matrix satisfying $CC^T = 10I_7 + 2J_7$ has weight 2, and we correct the support labels of the three $f=1$ branch representatives. A 12.5-day campaign on the $f=1$ and fixed-point-free order-3 encodings produced no satisfying assignment and no refutation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Lean~4 Formalization and Operational Taxonomy}

To prevent ambiguity between mechanized proofs, empirical solver outputs, and open conjectures, we classify every result and artifact into one of four operational states:

\begin{description}
 \item[\textsc{Proved}.] A theorem verified by the Lean~4 kernel using only the foundational axioms \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}; or a SAT claim for which the cited CNF is the file that was checked, its solver log ends in \texttt{s UNSATISFIABLE}, and \texttt{drat-trim} reports \texttt{s VERIFIED} on that pair. A checker log for a different formula does not meet this state.
 \item[\textsc{Compiled}.] Formally typed Lean~4 code compiling with \texttt{sorryAx} stubs for open subgoals; or Python SAT compilers whose clausal outputs pass deterministic regression tests.
 \item[\textsc{Explored}.] Combinatorial searches, matrix calculations, and multi-threaded solver executions that finished without finding a satisfying assignment or producing a proof certificate.
 \item[\textsc{Pending}.] Open mathematical questions (such as graph existence or full rigidity $|\Aut(G)| = 1$) and unfinished formalizations connecting group actions to arithmetic hypotheses.
\end{description}

\subsection{Kernel-Verified Structural Properties in Lean~4}

We formalize the adjacency definition of a Conway 99-graph in Lean~4~\cite{moura2021lean4} with the predicate \texttt{ConwayAdj}. This predicate enforces symmetry, zero diagonal, binary entries, and the matrix equation~\eqref{eq:matrix}. To support verified computation, we define a computable Boolean function \texttt{checkConway} and prove its equivalence to \texttt{ConwayAdj}:
\begin{equation}
 \texttt{conway\_soundness}, \texttt{conway\_complete}: \quad \forall A \in \mathbb{N}^{99 \times 99}, \quad \texttt{checkConway}(A) = \texttt{true} \iff \texttt{ConwayAdj}(A).
 \label{eq:checker}
\end{equation}

In the modules \texttt{Structural.lean} and \texttt{Z2Classification.lean}, we prove structural invariants directly from \texttt{ConwayAdj}:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \texttt{conway\_k4\_free} (\texttt{Structural.lean}): Every graph satisfying the matrix equation contains no complete subgraph $K_4$, because $\lambda = 1$.
 \item \texttt{conway\_neighborhood\_one\_factor} (\texttt{Structural.lean}): For each vertex $x$, the open neighborhood $G[\Gamma(x)]$ is a 1-regular graph of seven disjoint edges ($7K_2$).
 \item \texttt{conway\_diameter\_at\_most\_two} (\texttt{Structural.lean}): Any two non-adjacent vertices have common neighbors because $\mu = 2 > 0$. Therefore, $\operatorname{diam}(G) \le 2$.
 \item \texttt{conway\_z2\_f3\_fixed\_points\_dichotomy} (\texttt{Z2Classification.lean}): If an involution fixes three vertices, their induced subgraph is either a triangle $K_3$ or an independent set $3K_1$.
\end{itemize}
The Lean kernel checked all these declarations with \texttt{\#print axioms}. They depend solely on \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}.

\subsection{The Boundary Between Arithmetic Refutations and Graph Hypotheses}

Character-theoretic analyses of automorphisms impose numerical constraints on fixed edge counts and eigenspace multiplicities. In the Lean library, we kernel-verify the arithmetic refutations of these systems:
\begin{itemize}
 \item For an involution with $f=5$ fixed vertices, the declaration \texttt{conway\_z2\_f5\_\allowbreak spectral\_\allowbreak formula\_\allowbreak contradiction} refutes the existence of an integer $\varepsilon_1$ satisfying:
 \begin{equation}
  (\varepsilon_1 = 18 \lor \varepsilon_1 = 15) \land (\varepsilon_1 \bmod 7 = 6).
  \label{eq:f5_arith}
 \end{equation}
 \item For $f=7$, \texttt{conway\_no\_z2\_f7\_\allowbreak formula\_\allowbreak automorphism} refutes:
 \begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_1 \in \{7, 10, 13\} \land (\varepsilon_1 \bmod 7 = 2).
  \label{eq:f7_arith}
 \end{equation}
 \item In \texttt{CesarzWoldarTheorems.lean}, terminal arithmetic contradictions such as $7a = 62$ or sums of even integers equaling 5 are verified using only \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}.
\end{itemize}

These arithmetic refutations are \textsc{Proved}. However, their wrappers (such as \texttt{conway\_no\_z2\_f5\_\allowbreak automorphism}) take the counting identities and spectral congruences as hypotheses. Deducing these arithmetic hypotheses directly from \texttt{ConwayAdj(A)} and an involution $t$ in Lean remains \textsc{Pending}. Likewise, the classification theorem \texttt{conway\_automorphism\_\allowbreak group\_\allowbreak restricted} uses \texttt{sorryAx} for open subcases, placing its status at \textsc{Compiled}.

\subsection{Kernel-Checked Reduction from an Order-7 Action to the Orbit Quotient}
\label{sec:z7_reduction}

Behbahani and Lam~\cite{behbahani2011strongly} excluded order 7 with orbit matrices. The Lean library now contains a kernel-checked form of the first step of such an argument: the passage from a graph and an automorphism to a quotient matrix.

Let $s$ act on $V(G)$ with orbits $O_0, \dots, O_{m-1}$ of sizes $n_i = |O_i|$. For $u \in O_i$, let $B_{ij}$ be the number of neighbors of $u$ in $O_j$. Because $s$ preserves adjacency, $B_{ij}$ does not depend on the choice of $u$. The Lean structure \texttt{Z7OrbitMatrix} requires $m = 15$, $n_0 = 1$, and $n_i = 7$ for $i \ge 1$. It also requires these conditions for all indices $i$ and $j$:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $\sum_{j} B_{ij} = 14$, $n_i B_{ij} = n_j B_{ji}$, and $(B^2)_{ij} + B_{ij} = 12\,\delta_{ij} + 2\,n_j$.
 \item $B_{ij} \le n_j$ and $B_{ii} \in \{0, 2\}$. If $i, j \ge 3$, then $B_{ij} \le 4$.
 \item $B_{0j} = 7$ if $j \in \{1, 2\}$, and $B_{0j} = 0$ otherwise. $B_{i0} = 1$ if $i \in \{1, 2\}$, and $B_{i0} = 0$ otherwise.
 \item $B_{11} = B_{22} = 0$ and $B_{12} = B_{21} = 1$.
 \item The pair $(B_{i1}, B_{i2})$ is $(2, 0)$ for $3 \le i \le 5$, $(0, 2)$ for $6 \le i \le 8$, and $(1, 1)$ for $9 \le i \le 14$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{theorem}[\texttt{conway\_z7\_induces\_orbit\_matrix}; \textsc{Proved}]
\label{thm:z7_reduction}
Let $A$ satisfy \texttt{ConwayAdj}. Let $s$ be a bijection of the vertex set $\{0, \dots, 98\}$ such that $s^7 = \mathrm{id}$, $s \ne \mathrm{id}$, and $A_{s(u)\,s(v)} = A_{uv}$ for all vertices $u$ and $v$. Then a matrix that satisfies \texttt{Z7OrbitMatrix} exists.
\end{theorem}

The Lean statement has no hypotheses other than those in Theorem~\ref{thm:z7_reduction}. It does not take an orbit partition, an orbit count, or a quotient matrix as input. The proof has these steps:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \texttt{Z7Cycles.lean}: each orbit of $s$ has size 1 or 7. The number of fixed vertices is congruent to 99 modulo 7. Thus at least one fixed vertex $x_0$ exists.
 \item \texttt{GraphLocalCounts.lean}: \texttt{ConwayAdj} gives degree 14, one common neighbor for adjacent vertices, and two common neighbors for distinct nonadjacent vertices. The unique common neighbor defines a perfect matching on each neighborhood.
 \item \texttt{Z7FixedGeometry.lean} and \texttt{Z7GraphFrame.lean}: $s$ moves every neighbor of $x_0$. The set $\Gamma(x_0)$ is the union of two orbits of size 7, and no edge joins two vertices of the same such orbit. The vertex $x_0$ is the only fixed vertex.
 \item \texttt{Z7OrbitEnumeration.lean}: a computable list contains one representative of each orbit. The list has 15 entries, because one orbit has size 1 and $(99 - 1)/7 = 14$ orbits have size 7. Indices 0, 1, and 2 hold $x_0$ and the two orbits in $\Gamma(x_0)$.
 \item \texttt{OrbitQuotient.lean} and \texttt{OrbitReindex.lean}: the matrix $B$ is well defined. It satisfies the three equations of condition~1 and the bound $B_{ij} \le n_j$. If $n_i$ is odd, then $B_{ii}$ is even.
 \item \texttt{Z7QuotientBoundary.lean} and \texttt{Z7RootedConstruction.lean}: conditions 3 and 4 follow from the structure of $\Gamma(x_0)$.
 \item \texttt{Z7CanonicalArithmetic.lean}: for $i \ge 3$, the quotient equation gives $B_{i1} + B_{i2} = 2$. The equations then force exactly three outer orbits of type $(2, 0)$, three of type $(0, 2)$, and six of type $(1, 1)$. They also give $B_{ii} \in \{0, 2\}$, and $B_{ij} \le 4$ for $i, j \ge 3$. An explicit permutation puts the outer orbits in the order of condition~5.
\end{enumerate}

For Theorem~\ref{thm:z7_reduction}, \texttt{\#print axioms} reports \texttt{[propext, Quot.sound]}. The new modules contain no \texttt{sorry}, no \texttt{native\_decide}, and no new axioms. The file \texttt{TestZ7Canonical.lean} computes orbit representatives for two finite permutations: a permutation of 99 points with one fixed point and fourteen 7-cycles, which gives 15 representatives, and the identity map, which gives 99. These checks test the definitions. They are not Conway graphs.

The theorem gives necessary conditions only. It does not state that a matrix with these properties comes from a graph. The statement that no \texttt{Z7OrbitMatrix} exists (\texttt{z7\_orbit\_matrix\_\allowbreak nonexistence}) is still a \texttt{sorry}. The Lean statement \texttt{conway\_no\_z7\_\allowbreak automorphism} uses it and therefore remains \textsc{Compiled}. The facts in the proof are not new. The contribution is a kernel-checked derivation that removes one unproved premise from the Lean path to an order-7 exclusion.

\section{Canonical SAT Encodings and Symmetry Breaking}

To explore automorphism classes computationally, we map the graph constraints under a cyclic automorphism group $\langle g \rangle \cong \mathbb{Z}_p$ into Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF).

\subsection{Orbit Parameterization and Crawford Symmetry Cuts}

Under the action of an automorphism $g$ of prime order $p$, the 99 vertices partition into $m$ orbits $\mathcal{O}_1, \dots, \mathcal{O}_m$, where $|\mathcal{O}_i| \in \{1, p\}$. The adjacency between two orbits of size $p$ is invariant under cyclic shifts. A circulant Boolean vector $c_{i,j} \in \{0,1\}^p$ parameterizes this adjacency, where $c_{i,j,k} = 1$ if vertex $0 \in \mathcal{O}_i$ connects to vertex $k \in \mathcal{O}_j$.

The strongly regular graph conditions translate into exact cardinality constraints:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{u \in \Gamma(x)} A_{u,v} =
 \begin{cases}
  14 & \text{if } x = v, \\
  1 & \text{if } x \sim v, \\
  2 & \text{if } x \not\sim v.
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We convert these pseudo-Boolean constraints to CNF with Tseitin variables. Exact cardinality constraints use PySAT sequential counters (\texttt{EncType.seqcounter}). The compilers used for this note do not emit sorting networks.

To prune isomorphic search spaces, we enforce symmetry-breaking predicates following Crawford et al.~\cite{crawford1996symmetry}. Let $\mathcal{G} \le \Sym(m)$ be the automorphism group preserving the orbit partition. For each non-identity generator $\pi \in \mathcal{G}$, we enforce a lexicographical leader constraint: the assignment vector $X$ must precede its permuted image $\pi(X)$:
\begin{equation}
 X \le_{\text{lex}} \pi(X).
 \label{eq:lex_leader}
\end{equation}
For variables $(x_1, \dots, x_n)$ and their images $(y_1, \dots, y_n) = (\pi(x_1), \dots, \pi(x_n))$, we introduce auxiliary variables $e_i \leftrightarrow (x_i = y_i)$ and enforce:
\begin{equation}
 (\neg x_i \land y_i) \lor (e_i \land (X_{i+1..n} \le_{\text{lex}} Y_{i+1..n})).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Compiler Verification and Defect Repair}

Translating graph constraints into propositional clauses requires careful validation. In prototype implementations, primary Boolean variables were 1-indexed (with ID 1 for $x_1$), while helper functions such as \texttt{get\_and\_lit} and \texttt{add\_card\_equals} treated integer \texttt{1} as the Boolean constant \texttt{True}. When encoding $x_1 + x_2 = 1$ via \texttt{add\_card\_equals([1, 2], 1)}, the compiler evaluated literal 1 as constant \texttt{True}. This produced the degenerate unit clause \texttt{[-2]}, which enforced $x_2 = 0$ while omitting $x_1$. The formula then admitted $(x_1, x_2) = (0, 0)$ and rejected $(0, 1)$.
\begin{example}[The Literal-1 Collision Defect]
\label{ex:literal1}
The collision of variable ID 1 with the Boolean constant \texttt{True} changes an exact-1 constraint on $\{x_1, x_2\}$ into the unit clause $\neg x_2$. This defect shows why compiler primitives require end-to-end truth table tests.
\end{example}

We refactored the compiler architecture to separate variable indices from Boolean constants. Variables are now allocated through explicit Tseitin gate managers, reserving index 0 as an internal sentinel.

To test the compilers, we keep a deterministic regression suite of 101 tests in \texttt{tests/}:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item \textbf{Compiler-Helper Unit Tests (20 tests):} Verifies truth tables for conjunctions, cardinality constraints, circulant orbit variables, and Crawford lex-leader cuts for $Z_7$, $Z_3$ fixed-3, and $Z_3$ FPF compilers.
 \item \textbf{Geometric and Input-Audit Tests (11 tests):} Evaluates 2,394 cardinality constraints symbolically against the coordinate model, checking branch cut consistency and index mappings.
 \item \textbf{Supervisor Process Tests (8 tests):} Validates bounded execution, process lifecycle management, wall-time enforcement, and proof byte limits.
 \item \textbf{Orbit-Pair Tests (5 tests):} Check the orbit-pair enumeration for the case $f=1$.
 \item \textbf{Positive-Control Tests (32 tests):} Run the production positive and negative controls of Section~\ref{sec:positive_controls} on Paley(9), check the Berlekamp--van Lint--Seidel construction, and test the clause-check harness. This group also keeps older tests of standalone toy encoders, coordinate geometry, and relaxed subsystems. Those older tests do not show that a complete production formula admits a known graph.
 \item \textbf{Orbit-Matrix Tests (25 tests):} Check the quotient-level order-7 encoder against small exhaustive cases and known quotients (10 tests), and check the run supervisor for that encoder (15 tests). The supervisor tests check model validation, checker integrity, and the treatment of \texttt{UNKNOWN} results.
\end{enumerate}
On 3 October 2026 all 101 tests passed on the integrated revision. Passing tests support \textsc{Compiled} status for the current compiler sources. They do not rewrite CNF files generated earlier, and they do not prove that those files express the intended graph constraints.

\subsection{Positive Controls on the Production Compilers}
\label{sec:positive_controls}

An \texttt{s UNSATISFIABLE} result cannot show that an encoding admits all graphs that it must admit. The literal-1 defect of Example~\ref{ex:literal1} made a formula too strong. A positive control applies the same compiler to a known graph with the same $\lambda$ and $\mu$. If the compiler removes that graph, the control fails.

Two production compilers now accept the vertex degree as an argument: the compiler for an involution with one fixed vertex (\texttt{build\_z2\_f1\_cnf.py}) and the compiler for a fixed-point-free automorphism of order 3 (\texttt{scripts/build\_z3\_canonical\_cnf.py}). The order-3 compiler also accepts the number of orbits. The values $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = 2$ stay fixed. With the default arguments, the outputs of both compilers did not change: the SHA-256 digests agree with the outputs of the unmodified compiler sources. The default formulas have $666{,}309$ variables and $1{,}508{,}157$ clauses for the involution, and $852{,}324$ variables and $1{,}860{,}024$ clauses for order 3. This comparison does not use the CNF files of earlier runs.

Two known graphs with $\lambda = 1$ and $\mu = 2$ are the controls:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Paley(9), which is $\mathrm{srg}(9, 4, 1, 2)$. For each of the two actions, the solver finds a model of the complete compiled formula. The decoded graph satisfies the strongly regular conditions, and the model satisfies all clauses.
 \item The Berlekamp--van Lint--Seidel graph, which is $\mathrm{srg}(243, 22, 1, 2)$~\cite{berlekamp1973strongly}. The script builds it as a Cayley graph on $\mathbb{F}_3^5$ from the description in~\cite{zaw2020deza}, and it checks $A^2 + A = 20I + 2J$ before use. The map $x \mapsto -x$ is an involution with one fixed vertex. A translation is a fixed-point-free automorphism of order 3. The known graph sets the primary variables. The check then finds values for the auxiliary variables such that all emitted clauses are true: $28{,}257{,}515$ clauses for the involution and $27{,}424{,}360$ clauses for order 3. The auxiliary variables are checked in disjoint batches, and the check rejects the reuse of an auxiliary variable from an earlier batch.
\end{itemize}
Negative controls also pass. If one primary value of the Paley(9) assignment is changed, the compiled formula becomes unsatisfiable. A truth-table test checks the literal-1 case of Example~\ref{ex:literal1} in both production compilers.

These controls are \textsc{Compiled}. They do not prove that a compiler is correct for all inputs, and each Berlekamp--van Lint--Seidel check uses one fixed graph. They do not cover the order-7 compiler of Section~\ref{sec:order7} or the order-3 compiler with three fixed vertices. No DRAT proof is involved. The two Berlekamp--van Lint--Seidel checks are not part of the default test run. The repository records their output in \texttt{tests/production\_positive\_control\_results.json}.

\section{Certified Order-7 CNF Refutation}
\label{sec:order7}

Behbahani and Lam (2011)~\cite{behbahani2011strongly} excluded automorphisms of order 7 by a computer-assisted orbit-matrix argument. Cesarz and Woldar (2025)~\cite{cesarz2025automorphisms} proved, without a computer, that $7 \mid |\Aut(G)|$ implies $\Aut(G) \cong \mathbb{Z}_7$. Thakkar and Severini (2026)~\cite{thakkar2026forced} nevertheless report a single-fixed-point $\mathbb{Z}_7$ CP-SAT run that remained \texttt{UNKNOWN} after 48 hours on 14 cores, and they describe that symmetric case as still open. Here we report a verified SAT refutation for the canonical order-7 encoding.

\subsection{Encoding Parameters and Formula Statistics}

Under a cyclic automorphism $g$ of order 7, the 99 vertices partition into:
\begin{itemize}
 \item Exactly 1 fixed vertex $x_0$;
 \item 2 orbits of size 7 in the neighborhood $\Gamma(x_0)$ (because $k = 14$);
 \item 12 orbits of size 7 in the second subconstituent $\Gamma_2(x_0)$ ($84$ vertices).
\end{itemize}
This decomposition matches the orbit structure of Cesarz and Woldar~\cite{cesarz2025automorphisms}. The canonical CNF encoding uses 498 primary variables: 36 internal orbit valences and 462 cross-orbit circulant variables. We encode degree, $\lambda=1$, and $\mu=2$ constraints into CNF and add Crawford lex-leader cuts for the 11 non-identity elements of the stabilizer $G_{\mathbb{Z}_7}$. The resulting formula emitted by the repaired compiler has:
\begin{equation}
 \text{Variables: } 177{,}097, \qquad \text{Clauses: } 422{,}508.
\end{equation}

\section{Incidence Geometry and Search Limits for Open Branches}

The literature cited above leaves two prime-order actions unresolved by this note:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item An involution ($p=2$) with exactly $f=1$ fixed point (Makhnev--Minakova);
 \item A fixed-point-free automorphism of order 3. Behbahani's fixed-point restriction does not remove it, and Crnkovi{\'c}--Maksimovi{\'c} do not exclude it when they exclude orders 6 and 9.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Incidence Geometry of the One-Fixed-Vertex Involution \texorpdfstring{($f=1$)}{(f=1)}}

Let $t \in \Aut(G)$ be an involution with $\Fix(t) = \{x_0\}$. The rooted neighborhood $\Gamma(x_0)$ consists of 7 matched pairs under $t$. The second subconstituent $\Gamma_2(x_0)$ contains 84 vertices partitioned into 42 transposed pairs (orbits $O_0, \dots, O_{41}$).

Each orbit $O_j = \{u, t(u)\}$ has a support in $\Gamma(x_0)$. This support is the set of matched pairs in $\Gamma(x_0)$ containing neighbors of $u$. We define the binary support-incidence matrix $C \in \{0, 1\}^{7 \times 42}$, where $C_{i,j} = 1$ if orbit $O_j$ connects to the $i$-th matched pair of $\Gamma(x_0)$. By construction, $C = [C_1 \mid C_1]$, where $C_1 \in \{0,1\}^{7 \times 21}$ is the vertex-edge incidence matrix of $K_7$.
Computing the product gives the matrix equation:
\begin{equation}
 C C^T = 10 I_7 + 2 J_7.
 \label{eq:cct}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Exact Column Weight Distribution]
For every binary matrix $C \in \{0, 1\}^{7 \times 42}$ satisfying $C C^T = 10 I_7 + 2 J_7$, each column has weight exactly 2.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $k_j = \sum_{i=1}^7 C_{i,j}$ be the sum of column $j$. The total number of ones in $C$ is $\sum_{j=1}^{42} k_j = \Tr(C C^T) = \Tr(10I_7 + 2J_7) = 70 + 14 = 84$.
The sum of squares of column sums equals the quadratic form:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=1}^{42} k_j^2 = \mathbf{1}_7^T (C C^T) \mathbf{1}_7 = \mathbf{1}_7^T (10 I_7 + 2 J_7) \mathbf{1}_7 = 10(7) + 2(7^2) = 70 + 98 = 168.
\end{equation}
The variance of $k_j$ around 2 is:
\begin{equation}
 \sum_{j=1}^{42} (k_j - 2)^2 = \sum_{j=1}^{42} k_j^2 - 4 \sum_{j=1}^{42} k_j + 4(42) = 168 - 4(84) + 168 = 168 - 336 + 168 = 0.
\end{equation}
Because $(k_j - 2)^2 \ge 0$ for all $j$, we have $k_j = 2$ for all $j \in \{1, \dots, 42\}$.
\end{proof}
Note that $\Tr(C^T C) = \Tr(C C^T) = 84$, while the quadratic form $\mathbf{1}_7^T (C C^T) \mathbf{1}_7 = 168$ gives the sum of squared column weights.

\subsection{Stabilizer Decomposition and Branching Classes}

The binary matrix $C$ allows independent swaps of its 21 duplicate-column pairs. This generates a relabeling group $(S_2)^{21} \rtimes S_7$ of order $10,569,646,080$. However, in the signed rooted coordinate system of the graph, the valid coordinate symmetry group is:
\begin{equation}
 W = (\mathbb{Z}_2)^7 \rtimes S_7, \qquad |W| = 2^7 \cdot 7! = 645,120.
\end{equation}
Fixing a distinguished orbit $O_0$ with support $\{0, 1\}$, the stabilizer $\Stab_W(O_0)$ has order 15{,}360 inside $W$. The global sign flip fixes every orbit label, so it lies in the kernel of the action on the 42 labels. The induced permutation group therefore has order 7{,}680, and the faithful stabilizer of $O_0$ has order 7{,}680. These orders were obtained by enumerating the stabilizer in the signed coordinate model. Under $\Stab_W(O_0)$, the remaining 41 orbits fall into three classes:
\begin{itemize}
 \item \textbf{Class A (opposite phase, identical support):} Size 1, representative $O_{21}$, support $\{0, 1\}$, phase $-$. The abstract residual order is 15{,}360 and the faithful residual order is 7{,}680.
 \item \textbf{Class B (intersecting support):} Size 20, representative $O_1$, support $\{0, 2\}$, phase $+$. Then $|\{0, 1\} \cap \{0, 2\}| = 1$. The abstract residual order is 768 and the faithful residual order is 384.
 \item \textbf{Class C (disjoint support):} Size 20, representative $O_{11}$, support $\{2, 3\}$, phase $+$. Then $\{0, 1\} \cap \{2, 3\} = \emptyset$. The residual orders are again 768 and 384.
\end{itemize}
An earlier draft assigned support $\{1, 6\}$ to $O_1$. That label is the support of $O_{10}$. Since $O_0$ has support $\{0, 1\}$, the index $O_{10}$ is another intersecting representative, in the same class as $O_1$, and it is not a disjoint representative. The representatives used here are $A=21$, $B=1$, and $C=11$. The input-audit tests check these indices against the compiler's edge list of $K_7$.

\subsection{Limits of Combinatorial Search: The Distributed Solver Campaign}

To test whether CDCL solvers can resolve the open branches without further theoretical reductions, we ran a multi-threaded search campaign using CaDiCaL on Google Cloud Platform (16 cores, dedicated instance). The search targeted the $Z_2$ ($f=1$) branches and the $Z_3$ FPF encoding.

The campaign ran for 12.5 days and ended on 23 September 2026. The conflict total below is the aggregate recorded for that run. The solver logs are not part of this source, and the machines have been shut down, so a reader cannot recompute the total from this file:
\begin{itemize}
 \item The solvers accumulated over \textbf{2.4 billion conflicts} ($> 2.4 \times 10^9$) across multiple random seeds and variable selection heuristics.
 \item The solvers found neither a satisfying assignment (graph witness) nor an unsatisfiable termination (refutation).
\end{itemize}
Two limits apply to the formulas of this campaign. First, the campaign started before the compiler repair of 21 September 2026, and we did not audit which compiler version wrote each formula. Some formulas can therefore contain the literal-1 defect of Example~\ref{ex:literal1}. Second, project records name $O_{10}$ as the representative of the disjoint class for at least part of the campaign. As shown above, $O_{10}$ is an intersecting representative. The campaign therefore may not cover the disjoint class.

Under our taxonomy, this campaign is classified as \textsc{Explored}. Conflict counts, clause counts, and proof sizes do not measure the proportion of the mathematical search space eliminated. The 12.5-day run ended without a satisfying assignment or a refutation.

\section{Conclusion}

This report records the following boundary. It does not decide existence.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item The Lean library contains kernel-checked structural invariants and arithmetic refutations. Counting hypotheses that are merely assumed remain \textsc{Pending}. All Lean modules are available in the public repository.
 \item The Lean library derives the canonical 15-orbit quotient matrix from a Conway 99-graph with an automorphism of order 7 (Theorem~\ref{thm:z7_reduction}, \textsc{Proved}). The nonexistence of that matrix is \textsc{Pending} in Lean, so the Lean statement that excludes order 7 remains \textsc{Compiled}.
 \item The current SAT compilers use Crawford symmetry breaking and sequential counters. A literal-index defect was repaired on 21 September 2026. The 101-test regression suite checks the current compiler sources. It does not repair CNF files written before that date. Positive controls on Paley(9) and on the Berlekamp--van Lint--Seidel graph exercise the production compilers for the involution with one fixed vertex and for the fixed-point-free action of order 3. They are \textsc{Compiled} and do not cover the order-7 compiler.
 \item The canonical order-7 CNF from the repaired compiler is \textsc{Proved} as a formula. CaDiCaL 1.9.5 returned \texttt{s UNSATISFIABLE} in $58{,}054.87$\,s process time, and \texttt{drat-trim} reported \texttt{s VERIFIED} on the $26{,}746{,}488{,}053$-byte proof in $103{,}482.837$\,s. The SHA-256 digests above match those two files in the published Zenodo record \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22995101}{10.5281/zenodo.22995101}. The earlier record \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22983776}{10.5281/zenodo.22983776} stores a $192{,}218{,}491$-byte proof from an earlier run. A bounded run on a quotient-level order-7 formula ended with \texttt{c UNKNOWN} and is \textsc{Explored}.
 \item Every binary $7\times 42$ solution of $CC^T = 10I_7 + 2J_7$ has column weight 2. In the signed model the three $f=1$ representatives are $O_{21}$, $O_1$ with support $\{0, 2\}$, and $O_{11}$ with support $\{2, 3\}$. The multi-threaded search remained \textsc{Explored}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The Conway 99-Graph and the Rigidity Conjecture.}
A central question is whether any Conway 99-graph must be rigid:
\begin{conjecture}[Rigidity Conjecture]
If a strongly regular graph with parameters $(99, 14, 1, 2)$ exists, then $\Aut(G) = \{1\}$.
\end{conjecture}
A search that assumes a nontrivial automorphism ($Z_2$, $Z_3$, or $Z_7$) excludes every rigid graph from its search space. Refuting the open $f=1$ and fixed-point-free order-3 branches would prove the rigidity conjecture and would leave existence open.
